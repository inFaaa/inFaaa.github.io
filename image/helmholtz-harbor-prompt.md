## Harbor

Use case: photorealistic-natural. A single extra-wide 8:3 nadir drone photograph of a working harbor basin for a small navigation simulation on a research homepage. The drone points straight down, exactly perpendicular to the surface, with no horizon or sky. Compose the entire photograph across one continuous stretch of deep blue-grey, faintly teal seawater. Along the TOP EDGE ONLY, show a narrow strip of old stone-and-concrete quay, a few photogenic rusty bollards and weathered warehouse roof corners to establish scale. Along the top at 45 percent of the picture width there is a patch of clear concrete quay edge where a narrow temporary pier can be attached later. All permanent shore structures stay within the uppermost 15 percent of image height. The entire central and lower 85 percent of the frame is uninterrupted open dark water, with subtle small sunless ripples, fine low-contrast olive-blue currents and physical detail. Documentary aerial photography, realistic textures, cool overcast morning, quiet restrained colors, low visual noise. The visual should still read as an aerial harbor photograph at a very small size. No boats, hulls, buoys, breakwaters, floating objects, wakes, labels, symbols, lines, watermark, map graphics, border, or lettering. Generate the panoramic composition as a single photo.

## Breakwater

Absolutely straight and vertical from top toward bottom, aligned with the canvas center. Bright natural daylight with faint shadows on the stones only. Readable as a newly imagined obstruction over dark teal water, even at a tiny 11 by 32 pixel display size. Show just this one object in full, cut out cleanly, transparent around every side. No water, horizon, base tile, adjacent shore, plants, railings, boats, person, effects, cast shadow outside the cutout, sign or text.
